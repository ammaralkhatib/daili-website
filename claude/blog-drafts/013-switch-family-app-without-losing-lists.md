# Switch Family Apps Without Losing Your Lists

Moving a family from one organiser app to another fails in a predictable way,
and it is almost never on day one.

Day one is fine — you are motivated, you enter things, it feels clean. Day three
is when somebody opens the old app out of habit, adds something there, and
nobody else sees it. By the end of week one there are two half-true calendars,
and the family goes back to asking each other, which is where they were before
any app existed.

The fix is not a better app. It is running the switch as a planned two-week
overlap instead of a clean break.

## Why the clean break fails

A clean break asks four or five people to change a habit on the same day, with
no fallback. Any one of them forgetting breaks trust in the new system for
everyone.

It also throws away your safety net at the exact moment you need one. If the new
app turns out not to fit, you want the old one still populated, not deleted.

## The two-week overlap plan

**Days 1–2: move the calendar.**

Most calendar apps publish a read-only feed (an ICS link) that another calendar
can subscribe to. If yours does, subscribe to it from the new app. Now the new
app shows the old app's events without anyone re-typing anything, and it stays
current while the old one is still in use.

If your old app has no feed, do not hand-type a year of events. Enter the next
six weeks and the fixed points beyond that — school terms, birthdays, holidays,
anything already booked. The rest was never going to be looked at.

**Days 1–2: move the lists by copying, not recreating.**

Shopping lists, to-do lists and recipes almost never export as a file. What they
usually do is print, or show on a web page you can select text from. Copy the
text out, paste it into a note, then paste it into the new app.

Two rules. Only move **active** items — a to-do list carries years of dead
entries and moving them poisons the new app on day one. And do the recipes you
actually cook, not all of them; most recipe boxes contain forty saved recipes
and eight that get made.

**Days 3–14: both apps stay installed, one is authoritative.**

Announce which one is real. Everything new goes in the new app. The old one is
open for reference only.

When somebody adds something to the old app — and they will — do not be annoyed,
just move it across and say so. Two weeks of that and the habit turns over.

**Day 14: the cut-over.**

Check three things before you commit. Has everyone other than you added at least
one thing? Do the next four weeks look right in the new app? Is anything still
only in the old one?

If yes to the first two, remove the old app from everyone's phone on the same
day. Leave the account alive for now.

**Day 30 or so: close the old account.**

Once you are sure, close it properly. Check whether deleting your account
deletes the whole family's data — many family apps work that way, and someone
else may still be relying on it.

## Moving birthdays without losing the years

A small thing that catches people out.

Birthdays exported as calendar events often come across as a repeating event
starting this year, and the birth year is lost. If the year matters to you — and
it does the moment somebody asks how old an aunt is turning — check a couple of
entries after moving, and re-enter the year where it has gone.

Recurring events in general are the ones to spot-check. A weekly fixture usually
survives; anything with an unusual pattern often does not.

## What you will lose, honestly

Some things do not come across, and it is better to decide in advance that you
are letting them go.

- **Completed history.** Old finished to-dos, past shopping lists. Nobody has
  ever missed these.
- **Photos attached to events**, in most cases.
- **Comments and chat threads** attached to items.
- **The exact structure** of nested lists or custom categories.

If any of those genuinely matter, screenshot them before you close the old
account. That is a five-minute job and it beats discovering it afterwards.

## Do the shopping list first

If you only do one thing from this article, move the shopping list before you
move anything else.

It is the feature with the most immediate payoff — everyone feels it within two
days, at the supermarket — and it is the easiest habit to establish. A family
that has successfully moved its shopping list will move its calendar. A family
that starts with the calendar often never gets to the list.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should I run both apps?**
Two weeks is enough for most families. Longer than three and you have two live
systems rather than a transition, which is worse than either app on its own.

**What if my partner keeps using the old one?**
Expect it for the first week — it is habit, not resistance. Move their entries
across and tell them you did, without making it a thing. If it is still
happening in week three, the problem is adoption rather than migration, and
that is a different conversation.

**Can I import a CSV of my lists?**
Rarely. Most family apps have no list import. Copy-and-paste from a printed or
web view is the realistic route, which is why moving only active items matters —
you are doing this by hand.

# The Mixed iPhone and Android Household

Most family-calendar advice quietly assumes everyone is on the same platform.
Plenty of households are not: one parent on iPhone, one on Android, a teenager
on whatever was cheapest, a grandparent on a tablet nobody can identify.

The built-in options are the ones that break first, and it is worth
understanding exactly where, because the failure is not obvious until you are
depending on it.

## Why the built-in options do not cross over

**Apple Family Sharing needs an Apple Account.** Apple's own requirements are
explicit: joining a Family Sharing group requires a personal Apple Account
signed in to iCloud. There is no Android app. An Android user cannot join, and
no workaround changes that — it is a design decision, not a gap.

**Google's family group is built around Google accounts.** When you create a
family on Google, Google automatically creates a calendar called "Family", and
family members can find it and make changes to events on it. That works on an
iPhone too, but through the Google account rather than Apple's own system.

So neither platform's family feature is a cross-platform family feature. Both
are a way of tying together people who already made the same phone choice.

## Option 1: One shared Google Calendar

The most common answer, and usually the right one for a mixed household.

Everyone gets a Google account (they may already have one), you create one
calendar, and you share it with edit rights. Android users get it natively.
iPhone users add the Google account under Settings → Calendar → Accounts, and
the calendar appears in Apple's Calendar app.

**What works:** viewing and editing events from both platforms.

**What Google says does not work** when you use a Google calendar inside Apple's
Calendar app, in Google's own words: email notifications for events, creating
new Google calendars, and Room Scheduler. The first is the one families notice —
if you rely on emailed reminders, they will not come through this route.

Using Google's own Calendar app on iPhone avoids those gaps, at the cost of your
iPhone-owning family members running a second calendar app.

**The real cost:** everyone needs a Google account, including children. Google's
minimum age for managing your own account is 13 in most countries, but it is 14
in Austria, 15 in France, and 16 in Germany, Ireland, the Netherlands and
several others. Below that, a child's account has to be created with parental
consent through Family Link. That is worth knowing before you promise a
twelve-year-old their own calendar.

## Option 2: A public or shared iCloud calendar link

If the household is mostly Apple and one person is not, you can share an iCloud
calendar outward.

The important limitation is the one Apple states plainly: **"People who
subscribe to your public calendar can view it, but can't change it."** iCloud's
own help puts it another way — only the calendar's owner can share and make
changes to a public calendar.

So a public link gives the Android user a read-only view. They can see the
family's plans. They cannot add the dentist appointment they just booked, which
means one person is still doing all the entering.

Apple does offer editing, but only through private sharing to another Apple
account — the "Allow editing" option, which lets the invitee create, edit and
delete events. That route is not open to your Android family member.

**Use this when:** one person genuinely only needs to see the calendar. **Do not
use it when:** you were hoping to stop being the only one who adds things.

## Option 3: Subscribe to a feed

Any calendar that publishes an ICS link can be subscribed to from both platforms.
On iPhone that lives in the Calendar app: Calendars → Add Calendar → Add
Subscription Calendar. On Android it is done through Google Calendar on the web.

This is how you get school term dates, football fixtures and public holidays
into a family calendar, and it works across platforms without anyone changing
accounts.

Two limits. Subscriptions are **read-only** by design — this is for calendars
somebody else publishes, not for your family's own plans. And the update is not
instant: a subscribed calendar refreshes when the receiving calendar decides to
check, not when the source changes. Google does not publish how often it
refreshes subscribed calendars, and in practice people report delays of many
hours. If something is time-critical, do not rely on a subscription to carry it.

## Option 4: An app that runs properly on both

The fourth option is to stop using either platform's family feature and use
something built to be cross-platform, where both phones are first-class and
everyone can add things.

The honest trade-off: it is another app, and everyone has to actually use it —
which, as anyone who has tried knows, is the hard part regardless of platform.

## The worse problem: lists and reminders

Calendars are the easy half.

Apple Reminders does not exist on Android. Google Keep and Google Tasks work on
iPhone but are separate apps rather than part of the system. Apple's shared
shopping list and Google's are two different worlds, and in a mixed household
the usual result is that the list lives in a messaging thread — which is why
somebody always buys milk twice.

If your household is mixed, the shopping list is usually the thing worth solving
first. It has an immediate, daily payoff, everyone feels it, and it is the
easiest habit to start.

## Which option fits which household

- **Mostly Android, one iPhone** → shared Google Calendar. Least friction.
- **Mostly Apple, one Android who only needs to look** → public iCloud link.
- **Genuinely split, everyone needs to add things** → shared Google Calendar, or
  a cross-platform app. The public-link route will not do it.
- **Anyone under the Google account age in your country** → a cross-platform
  family app avoids the account problem entirely.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an Android user join Apple Family Sharing?**
No. Apple requires a personal Apple Account signed in to iCloud, and there is no
Android client. This is not a setting you have missed.

**Why does a shared calendar take so long to update?**
If it is a *subscribed* calendar (an ICS feed), the receiving app only refreshes
periodically, and Google does not publish how often. If it is a genuinely shared
calendar where you have edit rights, updates should be quick — so a long delay
usually means you are subscribed to a feed rather than sharing a calendar, which
are different things.

**Do we all need the same app?**
For a calendar everyone can add to, effectively yes — everyone needs to be in
the same system, whether that is a Google calendar or a family app. What you do
not need is the same phone.

### 🔴 Three things deliberately NOT stated — do not "improve" them in

1. **No claim that Apple auto-creates a "Family" calendar.** Apple's macOS 11
   documentation said it did; **no current Apple page mentions a Family Sharing
   calendar at all**, and the feature pages list only subscriptions, payments,
   parental controls and location. So the post says Family Sharing needs an Apple
   account and stops there. If someone wants that claim in, it must be checked on
   a current device first.
2. **No refresh-interval number for subscribed calendars.** The widely repeated
   "12 hours" traces only to a user forum thread title. The post says Google does
   not publish an interval and people report many hours — which is true and is
   all we can support.
3. **No mention of `calendar/syncselect`.** The claim that shared Google
   calendars must be enabled there before they appear on iOS is forum-only and
   absent from Google's help pages.

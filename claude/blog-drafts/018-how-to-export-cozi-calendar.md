# How to Export Your Cozi Calendar

There is no export button in Cozi. Not a hidden one, not a paid one — Cozi's
support pages document five getting-started guides, a FAQ and a contact form,
and none of them describes exporting or downloading your data.

That is not unusual for a family app, and it is not a scandal. But it does mean
that if you are leaving, the advice you will find in a dozen "best Cozi
alternatives" posts — all of which skip this part — is incomplete.

Here is what actually exists.

## What Cozi lets you take, and what it does not

**Your calendar: yes, as a read-only feed.** Cozi publishes an outbound iCal
URL that another calendar can subscribe to. This is documented and supported.

**Your shopping lists, to-do lists and recipes: no file export at all.** Cozi
documents printing — from the website, signed in at my.cozi.com, by month on one
sheet or by week as a list. The getting-started pages for shopping lists and the
recipe box do not mention export, download, email or CSV anywhere. Print is the
route, and it is web-only.

**Everything, in a machine-readable file: by request.** More on that below, and
it is the option most people do not know they have.

## The calendar feed: the documented route

Cozi's own help page for using Cozi with other calendars gives the path:

1. Sign in at **my.cozi.com** or in the mobile app
2. **Settings → Shared Cozi Calendars**
3. Switch a family member from **Unshared** to **Shared**
4. **VIEW OR SEND COZI URL**
5. **COPY COZI URL**

You can take one combined URL for "All", or a separate URL per family member.

Then subscribe to that URL from wherever you are going. In Google Calendar on
the web: **Other calendars → + → From URL**. On an iPhone: **Calendar →
Calendars → Add Calendar → Add Subscription Calendar**.

**Two things Cozi states plainly about this feed**, and both matter.

It is **read-only**. Cozi's wording: you can share appointments from Cozi to
another calendar program "in a read-only format". You will be able to see your
events somewhere else. You will not be able to edit them there.

It is **not instant**. Cozi says changes are available immediately in the
outbound URL, but "won't be reflected in the receiving internet calendar until
that calendar next checks for changes" — and the receiving calendar decides
when that is.

## A subscription is not an export

This is the distinction that the alternatives posts blur, and it is the whole
reason to read this article.

Subscribing to that URL does not give you a copy of your data. It gives you a
live window onto data that still lives in Cozi. If you close your Cozi account,
the window goes dark and the events go with it.

So a subscription is a **bridge**, not a move. It is genuinely useful — it lets
you run a new app alongside Cozi for a couple of weeks without re-typing
anything, which is exactly how a switch should be done. But at some point you
have to decide what you actually want to keep, and put that in the new app
properly.

## The route nobody mentions: ask for your data

Cozi's privacy policy, updated 11 June 2026, grants data portability in the
standard wording: the right to request the transfer of your personal data to you
or a third party in "a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format", and
to access, copy, update and delete your personal information.

Requests go through Cozi's contact form with **"Privacy" in the subject line**.
The policy states they will respond and stop processing within **28 days** of
receiving the request.

There is no self-service button, so this is an email and a wait rather than a
download. But it is a legal right, not a favour, and it is the only route that
covers the lists and recipes the feed leaves behind. If you have years of
recipes in there, it is worth the email.

## What to do about lists and recipes

Realistically, most people will not wait 28 days. The practical route:

**Print to a web page, then copy the text.** Signed in at my.cozi.com, use the
print view, and copy the text out of it rather than putting it on paper. Paste
into a note, then into the new app.

**Move only what is alive.** A to-do list that has been running for three years
is mostly dead entries. Moving them poisons a new app on day one.

**Move the recipes you actually cook.** Most recipe boxes hold forty saved and
eight made.

## Delete the account properly, afterwards

Not on the same day — leave it alive while the new app beds in.

When you do close it, know this first: Cozi's FAQ warns that deleting an account
deletes it for everyone in the family. If your partner is still using it, or the
teenager has their own events in there, closing your account is not a private
decision.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Cozi have a real export button?**
No. Cozi's support site documents no export, download or backup feature. The
outbound iCal URL is the only self-service way to get calendar data out, and it
is a live read-only feed rather than a file.

**Will my recurring events survive the feed?**
Cozi's documentation does not say either way, and we have not been able to
confirm it. Check a repeating event in the receiving calendar before you rely on
it — and if you have checked, we would genuinely like to know.

**Can I keep both apps running for a while?**
Yes, and you should. Subscribe to the feed from the new app, run both for about
two weeks, and only then close Cozi. That overlap is what stops a family
reverting on day three.

## PUBLISHING NOTES (delete before publishing)

### 🔴 HOLD — two things must be checked on a real free Cozi account first

Everything else in this post is sourced from Cozi's own pages (verified
2026-09-02 and 2026-09-11). These two are not, and they change the advice:

1. **Does the free 30-day calendar limit also apply to the outbound iCal feed?**
   If it does, the "bridge" only carries a month and the whole subscription
   route is much weaker. The article currently does not claim either way — but
   it should say so once known.
2. **Do recurring events come through the feed** — as repeating events, as
   individual dated copies, or not at all? The FAQ currently answers honestly
   that we do not know. That is publishable, but knowing is better.

How to check, on a throwaway free account: add an event **two months out** and a
**weekly repeating** event, copy the Cozi URL by the path in the article,
subscribe from Google Calendar on the web, and look.

**If the checks come back, update the FAQ and the feed section, and lift the
HOLD.** If they cannot be done within a week or two, publish as written — the
honest "we could not confirm this, check it yourself" is still better than the
nine competitor posts that assert things nobody tested.

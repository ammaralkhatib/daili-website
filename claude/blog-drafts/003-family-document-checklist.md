# The Family Document Checklist: 27 Papers Every Household Should Have Ready

Most document checklists are written for a disaster. This one is written for a
Tuesday.

The papers below are the ones a household actually reaches for: at a hospital
desk, at a school office, on the phone to an insurer, at a bank, at a border.
Twenty-seven of them. Some you need the original of and some you do not, and
knowing which is which saves more time than owning a bigger filing cabinet.

Work through it once. It takes an evening. Then read the last section, which is
the part that decides whether the list is still true next year.

## How to use this list

Against each item, do three things.

**Find it.** If you cannot put your hand on it in five minutes, that is the
finding — not a failure.

**Decide where it lives.** Originals go where they survive a fire or a flood.
Copies go where you can reach them in seconds. Those are two different places
and neither one does both jobs.

**Photograph it if a copy is allowed.** Not everything can be replaced by a
copy, and the next section says which.

## Which ones need the original

Germany's civil protection office sorts household documents into three tiers,
and the split holds up in any country.

**Original only.** Birth, marriage and death certificates, or the family record
book. These prove legal status and authorities want the real thing.

**Original or a certified copy.** Contracts, insurance policies, pension
statements, tax assessments, qualifications, wills, advance directives and
powers of attorney.

**A simple copy is fine.** Identity documents, passports, driving licence,
vehicle registration, vaccination records, property records, employment
paperwork and memberships.

That third tier is the group worth photographing tonight. It is also, not by
coincidence, the group you need at short notice.

## Identity — 7 documents

1. **Birth certificate** for every family member
2. **Passport** for every family member — note the expiry dates while you are
   there; family passports rarely expire on the same day
3. **National ID card or driving licence**
4. **Marriage certificate**, or the family record book
5. **Divorce decree or custody order**, if either applies
6. **National insurance, social security or tax ID numbers**
7. **Residence or visa documents**, if they apply to anyone in the household

## Medical — 5 documents

8. **Health insurance card** for every family member — the card itself, front
   and back
9. **Vaccination record** for every child, and for adults if you have it
10. **One allergy and medication page per person**: allergies, current
    medication with doses, and anything a stranger would need to know in an
    hour. Write this one; it does not exist as a document until you make it
11. **Repeat prescriptions**, current
12. **Contact details for the GP, dentist and any specialist**

Item 10 is the single most-used thing on this list, and the only one you have
to create yourself. Do that one first.

## Money and insurance — 6 documents

13. **Home and contents insurance policy**, with the claims phone number
    written on it
14. **Health insurance policy** (the policy, not just the card)
15. **Life insurance policy**, if you have one
16. **Car insurance certificate**
17. **A list of bank accounts** — bank name, account number, and the bank's
    phone number. **Never passwords, never full card numbers.** This list is
    so you can phone them, not so you can spend
18. **Pension statements**, most recent

## Property and vehicle — 4 documents

19. **Deed, or the tenancy agreement**
20. **Mortgage or loan agreement**
21. **Vehicle registration or logbook**
22. **A recent utility bill.** It sounds trivial until an institution asks for
    proof of address and you need one from the last three months

## School and childcare — 3 documents

23. **School enrolment paperwork and the emergency contact form** you filled in
24. **Childcare or nursery contract**
25. **A written consent letter** naming any adult who may collect your child or
    authorise treatment. Grandparents, a neighbour, an au pair

## The two nobody remembers

26. **Photographs of the inside of every room.** Ten minutes with a phone, once
    a year. If you ever make a contents insurance claim, this is the difference
    between what you can prove and what you can only remember. Open the
    cupboards.

27. **A list of who has a key** — and who has access to what. The neighbour, the
    cleaner, the landlord, the parent who moved out. This list is never written
    down anywhere and everyone assumes they remember it.

## Print it, or store it

You do not need all twenty-seven in your pocket. You need three things in three
places.

**The originals**, together, in a rated safe or a bank box. Tiers one and two.

**The instant set** on your phone, offline: items 8, 9, 10, 12, 25, and the
photo page of each passport. Six things. That is the set that answers a phone
call from a school.

**Everything else** in a cloud folder or a filing drawer — findable, but not
urgent.

If you want the fastest possible version of this whole article: do item 10,
photograph the insurance cards and vaccination records, and stop. That is
eighty percent of the benefit in fifteen minutes.

## Keeping it true

A checklist completed once and never revisited is worse than no checklist,
because you will trust it.

Put a repeating twenty-minute appointment in the family calendar, once a year,
on a date you already remember — the start of the school year works well. Three
jobs: replace what expired, add what is new, and check that one other adult can
still find everything without asking you.

That last one is the whole point. If you are the only person who knows where
things are, the system fails in exactly the situation it was built for.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I update this?**
Once a year is enough for the list as a whole. Two things need updating the day
they change: the medication page and anyone's insurance card. Everything else
can wait for the annual pass.

**What if I am renting?**
Items 19 and 20 become the tenancy agreement and your deposit paperwork, and
contents insurance matters more, not less — the building is insured by the
landlord, everything inside it is not. The rest of the list is unchanged.

**Do I need originals, or are copies enough?**
It depends on the tier. Certificates need originals. Contracts, policies and
wills need an original or a certified copy. For everything in the third tier a
plain copy does the job, which is why that tier is the one worth having on your
phone.
